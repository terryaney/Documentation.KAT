What's Happening

The list is built from the same facts the rail badges count, so a badge and its rows cannot disagree. There are two exceptions. The Release Pipeline badge counts approvals you can make, but approvals do not get rows of their own; they appear as chips on the release that has just deployed up to them. And a query set to Exclude from What's Happening (Settings > Work Items) still badges, but lists nothing.

Some rows describe a state: they stay while it is true and go when it is not. Others are news: they have a × and end on their own.

Actions only work on the selected site. A row about another site never shows approval chips; clicking it selects that site and opens the section. More than five rows of one kind fold into a single row that opens their section, and its tooltip lists the first ten. Rows for builds and releases the app is watching are never folded.

Colour in the list

  • The left edge is the row's tone:
    • red something is broken: a failed stage or build, a failing site, an unavailable section, or critical or priority 1 items over their threshold.
    • amber something waits on you: a checkout, or high severity, priority 2 or old items. A checkout turns red once it is older than Checkout alerts (Settings > Display).
    • blue it is in flight.
    • green it has just deployed.
    • No edge means it is for your information only.
  • The icon is the rail icon of the section the row belongs to, in that section's colour.
  • Environment chips are coloured exactly as they are on Release Pipeline: the environment's own colour once deployed or waiting for approval, red when failed, blue while deploying, and grey while queued.
  • Red text on a row means an action you took was refused. Hover the row to read TFS's message.

Rail badges

A badge counts what in its section needs you and will still be true after a refresh. A badge is never grey: it takes the worst tone among the things it counts, using the colours above.

Rail entry Counts
Each work item query The items that trip any of its enabled thresholds, each counted once. Red if a critical or priority 1 threshold is reached, otherwise amber.
Release Pipeline Approvals you can make (amber), plus failed stages no newer release covers and failed or cancelled builds (red).
CalcEngine Pipeline Every _PROD checkout, whoever holds it, and your own other checkouts. Amber, turning red past Checkout alerts.
Site Scope Non-QA sites that are failing (red). Warnings do not count.
Dashboard, Release Differences, Site Metrics, Diagnostics Never badge.
  • ! means the section failed to load or is not signed in. Hover it to see why.
  • A group (Work Items, Releases, Sentinel) shows the total of its children, in their worst tone, only while those children are hidden: collapsed, in icons mode, or combined into one page.
  • Badges for the selected site (Release Pipeline and CalcEngine) clear the moment you change site, and come back when the new site loads.
  • In icons mode a badge becomes a coloured dot.
  • Three animated dots on an entry are not a badge. They mean it is being checked: a query refreshing in the background, or Release Pipeline watching a build or release in flight.

Diagnostics

The last entry in the rail shows how the latest refresh went. Its tiles give the headline: Total refresh time, HTTP calls made, Sum of call time, Concurrency (sum of call time divided by total refresh - how much of the work ran side by side), and Cache hits and misses. Below them, Command Center Details names the TFS account the app is running as and where the settings file is, and Trace lists every step and call, nested under the step that made it.

Reporting a problem? Diagnostics has two buttons in its header for sending the KAT team what you are seeing. Save Diagnostics saves the refresh log as a JSON file, and Save HTML saves the current screen exactly as it is. Each shows where it saved the file. Attach the file when you contact the KAT team.

Sites with a release in flight

The site picker answers one question about every site at once, without you having to select them one at a time: has its newest release finished publishing to every environment? If it has not, the site carries an amber dot - on its row in the dropdown, and on the closed picker when it is the site you are looking at.

Typing * filters the list to sites carrying a dot. Anything you type after it still has to match as usual, so *sample means outstanding and matches "sample" - not either one on its own. The * only does this at the front of what you type; it is a mode switch, not a wildcard.

The dots are filled in by a background sweep that runs shortly after the app starts and continues to periodically poll in the background. It never blocks anything you are doing: sites light up as their answers arrive, and refreshing a site updates its own dot immediately from data that refresh already fetched. A site the sweep cannot reach keeps whatever dot it had rather than going dark, because a failed call is not evidence that a release finished.
